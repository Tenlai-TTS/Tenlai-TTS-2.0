Tenlai-TTS 2.0: Continuous-Representation LLM-Based TTS System with End-to-End Training

Abstract

Autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) with continuous speech latents avoids quantization-induced information loss, but typically relies on a latent space that is learned separately and then frozen before language-model training. Such a space must satisfy two competing requirements: it must retain enough acoustic detail for high-fidelity reconstruction, yet be sufficiently structured and regularized to support autoregressive prediction by a language model.

We present Tenlai-TTS 2.0, which learns continuous speech representations through end-to-end training to balance acoustic detail with autoregressive predictability. The model comprises a speech encoder with a capacity-controlled Gaussian bottleneck, a large language model (LLM) with an autoregressive diffusion transformer (AR-DiT) head, a flow-matching (FM) mel-spectrogram decoder, and a multi-task recognition model (RM). With energy normalization and fixed-variance Gaussian noise, the bottleneck has a closed-form upper bound on its information rate. Through joint optimization with reconstruction, recognition, and next-patch prediction objectives, we learn continuous speech representations that suit all three tasks and train the LLM and AR-DiT for autoregressive generation. The LLM and AR-DiT are further optimized using differentiable reconstruction and recognition feedback on their predicted latents through the FM decoder and RM.

On Seed-TTS-eval, this feedback-based optimization reduces character error rate (CER) from 0.82% to 0.74% on test-zh and from 8.32% to 7.08% on test-hard-zh, and word error rate (WER) from 1.32% to 1.27% on test-en, compared with joint training alone. Subsequent reinforcement learning further reduces CER to 6.29% on test-hard-zh and WER to 1.08% on test-en, with speaker similarity scores of 0.811, 0.766, and 0.784 on test-zh, test-en, and test-hard-zh, respectively.
