Tenlai-TTS 2.0 / Technical report

End-to-End Training for Continuous-Representation LLM-Based Text-to-Speech

Abstract

Autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) with continuous speech latents avoids the information loss of quantization, but its quality is bounded by a latent space that is usually learned in a separate stage and then frozen. Such a space has to serve two conflicting goals: it must retain enough acoustic detail for high-fidelity reconstruction, yet be sufficiently structured and regularized to support autoregressive prediction by a language model.

We present Tenlai-TTS 2.0, which learns continuous speech representations through end-to-end training to balance acoustic detail with autoregressive predictability. The model combines a speech encoder with a capacity-controlled Gaussian bottleneck, an LLM with an autoregressive diffusion-transformer (AR-DiT) head, a flow-matching (FM) mel decoder, and a multi-task recognition model (RM). Energy normalization and fixed-variance Gaussian noise give the bottleneck a closed-form information-rate upper bound. The encoder is first trained jointly with reconstruction, recognition, and next-patch prediction objectives, allowing the model to find a representation that suits all of them. We then optimize the LLM's latent predictions using differentiable feedback from reconstruction and recognition objectives, evaluated through the FM decoder and a frozen RM.

On Seed-TTS-eval, feedback-based optimization reduces CER from 0.82% to 0.74% on zh test set and from 8.32% to 7.08% on hard-zh test set, while reducing WER from 1.32% to 1.27% on en test set, compared with the stage of joint training. Subsequent reinforcement learning further reduces the CER to 6.29% on hard-zh test set and the WER to 1.08% on en test set, with speaker similarity scores of 0.811, 0.766, and 0.784 on the zh, en, and hard-zh test sets, respectively.
